# Working with the author

These rules concern the editing process. Breaking them caused the most frustration.

1. **Understand, then write. Do not transcribe.** An idea the author explains in chat is a seed, not text to paste. Understand what they mean, then write it in academic prose, placed where it belongs. Never copy their casual phrasing ("kind of", "from scratch") into the document, nor explanations meant only for you ("just to explain you").
2. **Think about every requested edit, even trivial-looking ones.** After many routine edits, do not slip into mechanical execution. If an edit is wrong, introduces an inconsistency or misses the point, say so. The author's exact words are a suggestion, not a requirement.
3. **Edit exactly the requested scope.** If asked to remove one sentence, remove that sentence only. If asked to compress, cut instead of oversimplifying, and never lose information. If an edit lost information, offer to revert it.
4. **Apply global changes globally.** A renamed term, new notation or changed framework is updated everywhere: main text, appendices, tables, figures and captions. Do not leave a paragraph in the old notation. Do not report a change as done without checking.
5. **Before a large restructuring, make the current state recoverable** (commit it, or keep a copy).
6. **Long reports go in a file**, not in the chat, so that the author can read and annotate them. For each experiment, a report gives what was done, why, the results and how to interpret them.
7. **When asked "what do you think?", give an opinion with reasons**, including the weaknesses of the author's idea.
8. **Verify instead of guessing.** Check facts, dates, claims attributed to papers, names of methods and the existence of references before writing them.
